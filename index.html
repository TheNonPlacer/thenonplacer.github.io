FAQFrequently asked questions
Ideal sizes
  • Cover: 1114 × 1654 px or larger, vertical (about 2:3).
  • Third page: 1015 × 1015 px or larger, square. You can simply upload the cover artwork again.
  • Photo / avatar: at least 400 × 400 px.
What works
  • Bigger images are always fine: they are scaled down automatically.
  • Square or horizontal images work too if they are large enough (for the cover, about 1500 px on the short side): they are cropped to the vertical format, and you choose the framing by dragging the image.
  • A resolution slightly below the ideal size is fine if the proportions are right (for the cover, from about 830 × 1230 px).
  • If the image is too small, the form tells you right away: export a larger version of the artwork and upload it again.
  • If you zoom in too much, the artwork would look blurry: the form asks you to reduce the scale.
Formats
  • JPG, PNG, WebP and PDF (first page) work in every browser.
  • HEIC photos from iPhone, TIFF and RAW files can't be read by most browsers: export them as JPG or PNG first. On iPhone you can also pick the photo from the Photos gallery: it is converted automatically.
  • Very heavy files (over 40 MB) can block the page on phones: export a JPG under 20 MB.
Still stuck?
  • Try another browser (Chrome, Safari or Firefox), or from a computer.
  • If you opened the page inside the Instagram app, use "Open in browser".
  • Or send everything by email to thenonplacer@gmail.com or in DM to @thenonplacer (photo or avatar, short bio, artwork with title and short description): we'll take care of the rest.
When a submission is sent successfully, you receive a confirmation email from thenonplacer@gmail.com within a few minutes. Check the spam and promotions folders and that the address you typed is correct. If after 15 minutes there's still nothing, the submission probably didn't go through: write to thenonplacer@gmail.com or in DM to @thenonplacer and we'll sort it out together.
All artworks will always remain fully attributed to the artist. On The Non Placer profile, every cover clearly includes the artist's name, their short bio and photo/avatar, and the title and concept of the artwork.

At this stage, artworks are used exclusively on Instagram. In the future, I would love to release an on-demand printed version of the magazine — but should that happen, all agreements will be re-evaluated together with the artists before any artwork is included. For now the magazine remains purely digital.
At the moment, no release form is required. The artist is not transferring or handing over their artwork. Each cover is simply a collaborative Instagram post, visible on both profiles — The Non Placer and the artist's. Authorship remains 100% with the artist, and The Non Placer acts more like a small contemporary art exhibition.
There is no cost and no obligation. If an artist ever wishes to remove their post, it can be archived immediately upon request via a simple message — no bureaucracy, no complications. It is, at its core, just an Instagram post.
The idea of a printed magazine is still distant and evolving. If one day The Non Placer becomes a print-on-demand publication, it would likely cost around €23–25 — simply covering printing and shipping. My intention would be: artists receive a free copy, while collectors, galleries and art buyers are the ones paying.

Traditional magazines cost over €40 plus shipping and often include only the artwork image, without bios or additional context. I want to change that by creating a more meaningful publication.
In the short term: build an authentic community, encourage collaboration, spark discussions, and generate positive word of mouth. Once we reach 25–50 artists, I would involve young journalists and the artists themselves to write short articles about art.

In the long term, I envision The Non Placer becoming an international platform supporting newly founded galleries and very young emerging artists.

Conceptually, the project draws inspiration from Italo Calvino's Invisible Cities and from the idea of negation: what is not done, not said, not seen, not known, not thought — whether architectural, abstract, or conceptual.
